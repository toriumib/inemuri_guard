TITLE (30 chars max)
Drowsiness Guard: Camera Alarm

SHORT DESCRIPTION (80 chars max)
Camera drowsiness alarm for desk or car. Stop it by voice, open eyes or a button.

FULL DESCRIPTION
Working, studying, on a night shift, on a long drive — and suddenly your eyes were closed.
Drowsiness Guard watches your eyelids and head position through your phone's camera and wakes you with sound, vibration, the screen and the light when signs of drowsiness persist.

STARTS THE MOMENT YOU OPEN IT
Prop up your phone with the camera facing you. Detection starts when the app opens.
It looks at two things: how long your eyes stay closed, and what share of the last minute they were closed. Blinks don't trigger it. Set the closed-eye time from 3 to 60 seconds (default 5).

WATCHES YOUR HEAD TOO
If your head slumps forward, tips back or falls sideways from your usual posture and stays there, it wakes you. Your "usual posture" is learned slowly, so it doesn't matter where the camera sits.
When the eyes can't be read (sunglasses, dark glasses) it shows EYES HIDDEN and watches head posture only.

KEEPS WATCHING IN OTHER APPS AND WITH THE SCREEN OFF
Detection continues when you switch apps or turn the screen off. The persistent notification only says "running"; the word "drowsy" never appears on your lock screen.

STOP IT WITHOUT TOUCHING THE PHONE
- Keep your eyes open for 3 seconds and it stops (for a head alarm, sit back up for 3 seconds)
- Say "okita" (I'm awake) or "stop" — uses your phone's speech recognition; can be turned off
- Press a volume button, the play button on your steering wheel or earphones, or the home button
- Tap "Stop" on the notification — no need to return to the app
- When you're looking at the screen, a big "I'm awake!" button appears

IN A CAR (set "Where you use it" to Car)
Keep Maps in front while it watches. When drowsiness is detected it tells you to rest at the next service area, parking lot or safe shoulder, and gives a gentle nudge if you keep looking to the side.
In a car, use it as an aid only and at your own risk. It does not replace the driver's duty of care. Do not handle the phone while driving, mount it securely where it doesn't block your view, and follow traffic law. If you feel sleepy, stop somewhere safe and rest — whatever the app says.

LIGHTS YOUR FACE IN THE DARK
If it's too dark to find your face, the screen turns white and bright to light it (can be turned off).

WAKES YOU QUIETLY, THEN LOUDER
Vibration first, then a quiet sound, then a loud one. With earphones in, the sound goes there.
Audio routing depends on your device; earphones-only playback isn't guaranteed. Follow local camera and device rules at work, in class or on public transport.

WAKES YOU WHEN SOMEONE CALLS
Optionally wake up on notifications from Slack, Teams, Outlook, Gmail, LINE or Discord, or when the phone rings (needs notification access). It only looks at which app the notification came from; message contents are never sent or stored.

NAP TIMER, POMODORO, HYDRATION
5/10/20/30-minute nap timer with research references, a Pomodoro timer, and a hydration reminder at the interval you choose.

LOG AND REFLECT
Detected events are logged on your device so you can see time-of-day patterns. Log your bedtime and wake time too. A sleep-improvement guide includes advice on seeing a doctor for persistent sleepiness; the app does not diagnose or treat any condition.

NOTHING LEAVES YOUR DEVICE
Eyelid and head analysis happens on your phone; camera frames are never uploaded or recorded, and the microphone is never recorded. "Stop by voice" uses your phone's speech-recognition feature, so audio while the alarm rings may be processed by the recognition service (can be turned off).
No account needed. Ads, in-app purchases and speech recognition use the network.

FULL-SCREEN INTENT JUSTIFICATION (if asked)
Drowsiness Guard is an alarm app that detects signs of drowsiness and wakes the user. When drowsiness is detected while the screen is locked, USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT is used to show the full-screen alarm (a large "I'm awake!" button) immediately, because a notification alone cannot wake a sleeping user.
